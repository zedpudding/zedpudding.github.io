Sunset Marquis had the rare problem every hotel says it wants: real history. Rock stars, writers, photographers, private villas, Bar 1200, Cavatina, the spa, and a mythology that guests could actually feel. The challenge was moving the brand forward without polishing away the reason people loved it.

I helped lead creative across the brand system for more than a decade: campaigns, printed pieces, signage, hotel collateral, restaurant and spa materials, web, retail, and the weird little guest-facing moments that decide whether a place feels legendary or merely expensive.

The work had to hold two truths at once. Sunset Marquis is a luxury hotel, but it is also a rock and roll hotel. The brand needed to feel elevated, lived-in, glamorous, private, funny, and just dangerous enough to still be interesting.

Not another hotel.

The work was never about making Sunset Marquis look newly expensive. It was about protecting the private, music-soaked, slightly dangerous energy of the place while giving the team a system that could work everywhere: room keys, menus, ads, web pages, signage, spa materials, restaurant collateral, and campaigns.
